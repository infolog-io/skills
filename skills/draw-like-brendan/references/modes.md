# Modes

## Sign or poster

Use one phrase and one central motif. Let the motif be disproportionately large. Add only marks that sharpen the joke or claim. Derive the background and fill colors from the closest source.

## Small diagram

Show one relationship with no more than three major objects. Good Brendan precedents include overlapping circles, two labeled towers, a central danger zone, a sign plus dice, or one object connected to one consequence. Labels can sit awkwardly around the drawing; they do not need a panel system.

## Sequence or workflow

Use 2–5 separate drawings or a loose strip. Give each beat one object/action and a short caption. Reuse one token across frames when useful. Avoid equal cards, numbered badges, dashboard symmetry, and dense footer bands unless a current-task original explicitly supplies that structure.

## Drawing array

Use when the concept has two or more meaningful dimensions: stages × actors, inputs × outcomes, system layers × moments, or scenarios × perspectives. Keep every cell to one visual proposition and at most three major objects. Render cells independently with the same 2–4 originals, then assemble them without redrawing. Rows and columns carry meaning; optional layers provide a third dimension. Use ragged gutters and open edges instead of card chrome. See `drawing-arrays.md`.

## Portrait

Keep one simplified figure dominant. Include only a few identifying objects. Preserve asymmetry and source-like flatness; do not turn the surrounding field into a sticker pack.

## Sketch redraw

Preserve the user's relationships and placement. Translate each shape through a close original drawing. Improve clarity only when doing so does not sterilize the source.

## Palette match

Borrow hard flat colors from the target while retaining Brendan's shape and lettering decisions. Do not import the target's UI language, gradients, or spacing system.
