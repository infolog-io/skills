08 · Charts

An effective chart decides six things.

Goal, marks, axes, descriptions, accessibility, color: the six dimensions of an effective chart. Color discipline is necessary, not sufficient. A chart with perfect color but the wrong mark, or a truncated axis, still fails. Tufte governs the ink; these six dimensions govern the decisions made before it.

G Goal

Decide the question before the mark. The cumulative line below answers one question (is the count growing?) in a form every reader already knows.

G01core-questionName the one question the chart answers before drawing. Cannot state it in a sentence? Not ready to chart.
G02design-for-focusOne chart, one variable cluster. Crowding three stories into a single frame buries all three.
G03familiar-formsPrefer bar and line. Novel forms (radial, sankey, packed bubbles) cost the reader decoding time they rarely have.
M Marks

Three marks, three jobs. The line in Goal traces a trend (M01); the bar below counts discrete months (M02); the dot plot ranks languages (M03).

M01line-is-trendLine marks encode rates of change and continuous trends. Never connect unordered categories; the slope implies a sequence that is not there.
M02bar-is-discreteBar marks encode discrete categories and cumulative sums. A bar tolerates a true zero, so a missing month reads as zero, not as a gap.
M03point-is-relationshipPoint marks encode relationships, clusters, and outliers across two properties. Avoid raw points for dense noisy data; they smear into ink.

The same marks shrink to sparklines — a compact vocabulary for tables and inline trends.

X Axes

The axis is where charts lie. The bar in Marks starts at zero (X01); the candlestick below frames a price range that does not (X02).

X01bars-start-at-zeroA bar's lower bound is zero. Truncating the axis inflates short bars and misstates proportion.
X02fixed-vs-dynamicFix the range when limits carry meaning, like 0 to 100 percent. Free it when small variations matter and zero would flatten them.
X03intuitive-ticksLabel with familiar increments, like 5, 10, 25. Avoid math-driven splits like 1, 6, 11.
X04sparse-gridFour to five horizontal grid lines. Go lighter and fewer when the chart supports tap-to-inspect.
D Descriptions

Words carry the chart. The cumulative line in Goal headlines its takeaway, then layers macro, medium, and micro in the caption beneath it.

D01headline-takeawayText above the chart states the primary insight. Do not make the reader derive the trend unaided.
D02macro-medium-microLayer context at three levels: macro is the whole dataset, medium is a meaningful subset, micro is one focus point.
A Accessibility

A chart no one can read is decoration. The specimen below keeps a thick focus ring on the selected bar and speaks its value as context first, then number.

A01context-then-valueLabels say "June 6, 850 installs," not "850." Spell it out: "June 6," not "6/6"; "60 minutes," not "60m."
A02visible-focusFocus rings are large, thick, and high-contrast against the marks they select.
A03input-parityEvery pointer gesture maps to keyboard, screen readers, voice input, and switch access.
A04audio-graphExpose each point to the platform audio layer for pitch-shifted playback. The readout below carries the aria note.
R Color

Color is the sixth decision, not the first. It enhances a chart that already chose its goal, mark, axis, and description; it never rescues one that did not. Green for up, red for down, blue for a reference or neutral mark, gray for everything else. Test each against color blindness, dark mode, and high-contrast modes.

Lint · six rules an LLM can apply

RuleNameLint
R01binary-directionEvery data mark is green (up · positive · growing) or red (down · negative · declining). No third hue on data.
R02symmetricThe rule holds across every chart. If one rising series is green, all rising series are green and all falling series red. No per-chart taste.
R03quiet-contextAxes and ticks use --gray-500; gridlines, frames, and labels stay quiet. Anything non-directional is gray, never colored.
R04reference-blueGreen up, red down. Blue (info) for a non-directional reference, baseline, or neutral mark. No yellow, ever.
R05latest-weightThe latest or highlighted mark may go heavier — thicker stroke, larger dot — but keeps its directional color. Weight draws the eye, not a special hue.
R06no-legendGreen-up / red-down is universal and needs no legend. Add a one-line caption only when direction is ambiguous.

Pass: every data mark is green or red by direction; everything else is gray. Every chart above obeys it — the slopegraph (rising green, falling red), the candlesticks (up green, down red), the sparklines (growing green, declining red). One rule, no exceptions.

A field guide to chart types

The six decisions, applied. Each chart below answers one question, drawn in the house style. Charts we can render honestly are real; charts that need data or compute we cannot reproduce here carry an illustrative tag — the honest-data-viz rule applied to its own limits.

Flow / stages— where does volume drop through a sequence?
Continuous XY— how does Y move over X, with what certainty?
Model fit & evaluation— does the model fit or classify well?
High-dimensional projection— what structure hides in many dimensions?
Relational— what connects to what?
Anything else— a chart the eleven do not name?

The guide is not a closed list. Any chart not shown — heatmap, treemap, sankey, choropleth (e.g. sales by state), radar, histogram, area — inherits the same six decisions and the house palette: quiet gray context, green up, red down, blue reference, and an illustrative tag whenever the data is not computed. 10 · Elevation & overlays

Three depth levels, encoded per theme.

Light leans on soft shadows. Dark drops them — shadows vanish on a dark page — and lifts instead with a lighter surface plus a hairline border. One set of tokens (raised, overlay, modal) drives cards, popovers, dialogs and toasts. A z-index scale keeps stacking order honest.

Raised .e-raised Cards, stat tiles, row hover. Barely off the page.
Overlay .e-overlay Popovers, menus, toasts. Clearly floating above content.
Modal .e-modal Dialogs over a scrim. The top of the stack.

z-index scale

--z-base0page content
--z-sticky50sticky topbar
--z-popover100menus, popovers, tooltips
--z-scrim1000modal backdrop
--z-modal1001dialog panel
--z-toast1100toasts — above everything

11 · Motion & animation

Three speeds, two curves — quiet by default.

Motion marks state changes and entrances. Nothing decorates. Every transition routes through duration and easing tokens. One media query flattens the whole system when the operating system requests reduced motion.
